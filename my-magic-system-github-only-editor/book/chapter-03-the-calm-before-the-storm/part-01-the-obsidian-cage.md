The transition from the blood-soaked dirt of the Underground Pit to the back seat of Elara
Vance’s

hover-limo

was

a

sensory

whiplash

that

Kaelen

wasn't

prepared

for.

The

vehicle

moved

with

a

ghostly

silence,

suspended

on

a

cushion

of

high-frequency

gravimetric

waves.

Inside,

the

seats

were

upholstered

in

genuine

leather

that

smelled

of

cedar

and

expensive

gin.

Kaelen sat by the window, his reflection ghostly against the reinforced glass. He looked like a
stain

on

the

perfection

of

the

interior.

His

tunic

was

torn,

his

knuckles

were

barked

raw

from

his

clash

with

Jaxen,

and

the

soot

of

Sector

4

seemed

to

have

permanently

etched

itself

into

the

lines

of

his

face.

Beside him, Elara Vance tapped away at a holographic interface. The blue light of her screens
danced

across

her

sharp

features.

She

didn't

look

at

him,

but

he

could

feel

her

scrutiny.

She

was

a

predator

who

had

just

found

a

very

strange,

very

dangerous

new

tool.

"You’re staring," she said, not looking up.
"I’ve never seen the city from this high," Kaelen replied, his voice sounding like gravel against
silk.

Outside, the "Grey Zone" was falling away. They were ascending the massive durasteel pillars
that

supported

the

Upper

Tier.

Below

them,

the

sprawling

slums

of

West

Salem

looked

like

a

glowing

bed

of

embers.

Above

them,

the

floating

citadels

hung

like

predatory

gods,

illuminated

by

artificial

suns.

"It’sa different world up here," Elara said, finally closing her screens. "One built on the
assumption

that

power

is

inherited.

You’ve

spent

your

life

mining

the

fuel

for

this

extravagance.

How

does

it

feel

to

be

sitting

in

the

engine?"

"It feels like it’s waiting to be broken," Kaelen said.
Elara’s lips thinned into a small, dangerous smile. "Good. Hold onto that. You’re going to need
it."

The limo passed through a shimmering violet veil—a Tier 6 security ward—and docked at the
Vance

Estate.

The

building

was

a

needle

of

obsidian

and

glass,

a

relic

of

a

time

when

her

family

held

the

keys

to

the

Council’s

armory.

Now,

it

was

a

silent,

empty

monument

to

a

fallen

legacy.

"This is your home for the next month," Elara said as the doors hissed open. "My staff has been
reduced

to

automatons.

They

don't

talk,

they

don't

gossip,

and

they

don't

have

biometrics

that

the

Council

can

subpoena.

You

are

safe

here,

Kaelen.

For

now."
