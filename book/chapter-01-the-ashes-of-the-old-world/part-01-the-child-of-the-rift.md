The air in the deep veins of Sector 4 didn't just smell like dust; it smelled like the slow,
pressurized

decay

of

human

potential.

Kaelen Nelson gripped the handle of his thermal-pick, the metal warm and slick with his own
sweat.

Every

rhythmic

swing

sent
testing commit
a

jolt

of

vibration

through

his

lean,

corded

frame,

rattling

teeth

that

had

grown

yellow

from

years

of

breathing

the

recycled,

metallic

air

of

the

underground

colonies.

At

twenty

years

old,

Kaelen

should

have

been

a

man

of

the

sun.

In

the

"Old

World"—the

era

his

grandfather

spoke

of

with

a

trembling,

nostalgic

voice—he

might

have

been

finishing

a

university

degree,

falling

in

love,

or

carving

out

a

career

in

a

city

made

of

glass

and

hope.

But Kaelen was a child of 2006. He had been born in the very hour the Anomaly tore the sky
open

over

the

Midwest,

a

cosmic

rift

that

bled

violet

light

and

rewrote

the

laws

of

physics.

They

had

called

his

generation

the

"Rift-Born."

Doctors

and

scientists

had

swarmed

the

hospitals

back

then,

predicting

that

children

born

during

the

event

would

be

gods

among

men.

They were half right. Magic had returned to the world, but it was selective. It chose the elite, the
lucky,

and

the

strong.

For

Kaelen,

the

"gift"

had

been

a

hollow

joke.

To the world, he was a Null. A genetic dead end. While the "Awakened" spent their days in the
upper

spires,

manipulating

the

fundamental

forces

of

the

universe

with

a

flick

of

their

wrists,

Kaelen

spent

his

days

twelve

hundred

feet

below

the

surface

of

West

Salem,

mining

the

very

mana-crystals

that

powered

their

paradise.

"Ninety-two... ninety-three..." Kaelen grunted, counting his swings.
The counting was the only thing that kept him sane. It turned the back-breaking labor into a
rhythm,

a

song

of

survival.

If

he

stopped

counting,

he

started

thinking.

And

if

he

started

thinking,

the

rage

would

become

too

much

to

contain.

"Hey, Null! Shut up with the counting! It’s giving me a headache."
Kaelen didn't look up. He didn't need to. He knew that voice—it was the sound of a man who
had

been

given

power

he

didn't

earn.

It

belonged

to

Barrett,

an

Awakened

overseer

whose

soul

was

as

jagged

as

the

cavern

walls.

Barrett’s

"Aspect"

was

Pyrokinesis,

though

a

weak,

stunted

version

of

it.

He

could

heat

his

palms

to

a

glowing

red,

enough

to

melt

steel

or,

more

often,

to

burn

"disciplinary

marks"

into

the

backs

of

the

miners

who

didn't

meet

their

daily

quota.

"Just finishing the vein, Barrett," Kaelen said, his voice a low, disciplined rasp.
"You'll finish when I say you're finished," Barrett sneered. He walked over, the heat radiating
from

his

body

making

the

stagnant

air

shimmer.

He

kicked

Kaelen’s

collection

bucket,

spilling

the

raw

mana-shards

across

the

dirt.

"Look

at

that.

Messy.

Pick

them

up.

One

by

one.

With

your

teeth."

The other miners, all Nulls with hollow eyes and sunken cheeks, looked away. They were men
who

had

been

broken

by

the

Tier

system

long

ago.

In

this

world,

there

was

no

labor

union

for

the

magicless.

There

was

only

the

hierarchy

of

the

Awakened.
